# Legal review: delay waiver, birth certificate waiver, vehicle ownership judgment

Reviewed 2026-10-02. Statute text below was fetched from legis.la.gov on that date and is quoted verbatim. Secondary sources (OMV Policy 3.00, Attorney General's Justice Court Manual 7th ed.) are named where relied on; they do not override statute.

Verdict key: SUPPORTED / OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG / UNCERTAIN.

Counts: SUPPORTED 26, OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE 10, UNSUPPORTED 2, WRONG 0, UNCERTAIN 0. Seven omissions are listed separately (O1 to O7); O1 is the most serious finding in this review.

---

## A. Waiver of the 24-Hour Delay (`forms/delay-waiver.typ`)

Deciding text, R.S. 9:241 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107088):

> "An officiant may not perform a marriage ceremony until twenty-four hours have elapsed since the issuance of the marriage license."

R.S. 9:242(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107089):

> "A. A judge, justice of the peace, or retired justice of the peace authorized to perform the marriage may waive the twenty-four-hour delay upon application of the parties giving serious and meritorious reasons. His certificate authorizing the immediate performance of the ceremony must be attached to the marriage license."

### D1. "A marriage ceremony may not be performed until 24 hours after the license is issued."
SUPPORTED. R.S. 9:241, quoted above.

### D2. "A justice of the peace authorized to perform the marriage may waive the delay when the parties give serious and meritorious reasons. R.S. 9:241 and 9:242."
SUPPORTED. R.S. 9:242(A). "Authorized to perform the marriage" is the statute's own qualifier.

### D3. Heading "Application of the parties", two names, two signatures, reasons.
SUPPORTED. 9:242(A): "upon application of the parties giving serious and meritorious reasons." Both parties apply. The statute does not require the application to be written; a written one is harmless.

### D4. Certificate: "The parties named above applied to me for a waiver of the 24-hour delay and gave serious and meritorious reasons."
SUPPORTED. Tracks 9:242(A).

### D5. Certificate: "I am authorized to perform their marriage."
SUPPORTED, with a condition. The phrase is used correctly: 9:242(A) gives the waiver power only to a justice "authorized to perform the marriage". The statute does not require the certificate to recite it, so the recital is optional. It is true only when these hold:
- R.S. 9:202 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=106877): "A marriage ceremony may be performed by: ... (2) A state judge or justice of the peace."
- R.S. 9:203(A)(5) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=106887): "A justice of the peace within the parish in which the court of that justice of the peace is situated, and in any parish within the same supreme court district, or in a parish that has no justice of the peace court, except for Orleans Parish."
- R.S. 9:205 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=106896): "An officiant may not perform a marriage ceremony until he has received a license authorizing him to perform that marriage ceremony."
- R.S. 9:235 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107076): "A marriage license is valid for thirty days from the date of issuance. No officiant shall perform a marriage after the license has expired."

Practice point, not a wording change: sign the certificate only with the unexpired license in hand and for a ceremony inside the 9:203(A)(5) territory. If the office prefers to recite nothing the statute does not require, the sentence can be omitted without loss; the signature block already states the office.

### D6. Certificate: "I waive the delay and authorize the immediate performance of the marriage ceremony between ___ and ___" with Date and Time.
SUPPORTED. 9:242(A): "His certificate authorizing the immediate performance of the ceremony". Date and time are not required by 9:242 but are consistent with R.S. 9:234(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107068), "The official who issues the marriage license shall show on the face of it the exact time and date of issuance", and R.S. 9:245(A)(2) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107116), "The marriage certificate shall show the place, time, and date of the performance of the ceremony."

### D7. "This certificate must be attached to the marriage license. R.S. 9:242."
SUPPORTED. Verbatim from 9:242(A). The statute does not say "each copy". The Manual (line 9053) says the waiver "must be in writing and attached to each copy of the marriage license"; that is secondary and goes beyond the statutory words. Do not print "each copy" as law. As office practice, attaching a signed certificate to each of the three copies is prudent because R.S. 9:245(B) says "Every officiant ... shall sign a certificate of marriage in triplicate" and R.S. 9:253(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107123) sends one copy to the parties and two to the clerk.

### D8. `forms.toml` title "Waiver of the 24-Hour Delay"; purpose "Ask the Justice of the Peace to allow a marriage ceremony less than 24 hours after the license is issued."
SUPPORTED. 9:241, 9:242(A).

### D9. `weddings.md` step 2: "The ceremony cannot take place until 24 hours after the license is issued. For serious and meritorious reasons, the Justice of the Peace may waive the wait; see the Waiver of the 24-Hour Delay form."
SUPPORTED. 9:241, 9:242(A).

### R.S. 9:243 to 9:245, 9:253
No bearing on the certificate's content. R.S. 9:243 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107095): "An officiant who violates R.S. 9:241, except for a judge, justice of the peace or an officiant authorized to perform marriage ceremonies in the parish of Orleans and who is authorized to waive the twenty-four-hour delay pursuant to the provisions of R.S. 9:242(B), may have his authority to perform marriage ceremonies revoked by the state registrar of vital records." 9:244 (witnesses), 9:245 (certificate content, triplicate), 9:253 (filing within ten days) do not mention the waiver.

## B. Waiver of Birth Certificate (`forms/birth-certificate-waiver.typ`)

Deciding text, R.S. 9:228(B) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107040):

> "B. In the event of extenuating circumstances, and for good cause shown, and after a hearing, which may be held in camera, finding that the parties have complied with all other requirements, including presentation of the letter required by R.S. 9:227 and other competent evidence that the applicant was born in any state or territory of the United States, a justice of the peace or city court judge may order an issuing official within the parish where his court is situated to issue a marriage license without the applicant submitting a birth certificate. The order need not state the reasons. The written order shall be attached to the marriage application."

R.S. 9:227 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107030):

> "A. If no birth certificate is on file for an applicant, a letter signed by the proper registration authority of the state, territory, or country of the place of birth of the applicant, under his raised seal or stamp, shall be submitted in lieu of a birth certificate. The letter must state that a thorough search was made and that no birth record was located for the applicant.
> B. The letter issued pursuant to Subsection A of this Section shall be filed with the court conducting the hearing pursuant to R.S. 9:228.
> C. The judge issuing the order waiving the birth certificate in order to obtain the marriage license shall demand other proof of birth facts."

R.S. 9:225(A)(1)(b) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107001): "If the applicant does not have a birth certificate, the applicant shall obtain an order signed by a judge waiving the requirement pursuant to R.S. 9:228."

### B1. Opening: "When an applicant for a marriage license who was born in a state or territory of the United States has no birth certificate on file, a justice of the peace may, after a hearing, order that the license be issued without it. R.S. 9:227 and 9:228."
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. Everything stated is true, including "born in a state or territory of the United States" (9:228(B): "born in any state or territory of the United States"). It leaves out two of the statute's conditions, "extenuating circumstances" and "good cause shown", and the territorial limit of the order ("an issuing official within the parish where his court is situated"). A resident could read it as routine.
Corrected wording: "When no birth certificate is on file for an applicant who was born in a state or territory of the United States, a justice of the peace may, in extenuating circumstances, for good cause shown, and after a hearing, order an issuing official in the parish where the court sits to issue the marriage license without it. R.S. 9:227 and 9:228(B)."

### B2. Attached: "A letter signed by the registration authority of the place of birth, under its raised seal or stamp, stating that a thorough search was made and that no birth record was located. R.S. 9:227."
SUPPORTED. 9:227(A), quoted above. Attaching it to the application also satisfies 9:227(B) ("shall be filed with the court conducting the hearing"), provided the court keeps the original in its record. Note 9:227(A) also says the letter "shall be submitted in lieu of a birth certificate", which reads as submission with the license application; whether the clerk wants the original or a copy is not settled by the text. Do not state a rule on the form; the court should keep the filed original and the applicant should ask the clerk.

### B3. Attached: "Other proof of the facts of birth:"
SUPPORTED. 9:227(C): "shall demand other proof of birth facts"; 9:228(B): "other competent evidence that the applicant was born in any state or territory of the United States". Both attachments are mandatory, but the form shows them as tick boxes, which can read as optional. Suggested lead-in under the heading: "Both are required."

### B4. "I ask the court to order that a marriage license be issued without a birth certificate."
SUPPORTED. 9:225(A)(1)(b), 9:228(B).

### B5. Order: "After a hearing, the court finds extenuating circumstances and good cause."
SUPPORTED. Tracks 9:228(B). "Hearing held on (date)" is a sound addition. The statute permits the hearing in camera; no recital is needed.

### B6. Order: "The court finds that the parties have complied with all other requirements, including presentation of the letter required by R.S. 9:227 and other competent evidence that the applicant was born in a state or territory of the United States."
SUPPORTED as wording. It is 9:228(B) almost verbatim ("a state" for "any state" is immaterial). See omission O2: the application gives the justice no record on which to find that "the parties" complied with "all other requirements".

### B7. Order: "IT IS ORDERED that the official who issues marriage licenses in St. Tammany Parish, State of Louisiana issue a marriage license to ___ and ___"
SUPPORTED. 9:228(B): "may order an issuing official within the parish where his court is situated". R.S. 9:221(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=106990): "A license authorizing an officiant to perform a marriage ceremony must be issued by: (1) The state registrar of vital records, or a judge of the city court, in the Parish of Orleans; (2) The clerk of court, in any other parish; or (3) A district judge, if the clerk of court is a party to the marriage." The form's generic phrase covers both (2) and (3) and is accurate. Closer to the statute, if preferred: "that an issuing official within St. Tammany Parish, State of Louisiana, issue a marriage license to".

### B8. Order: "without the applicant submitting a birth certificate. R.S. 9:228(B)."
SUPPORTED. Verbatim. Subsection B is the correct subsection for a justice of the peace; subsection A covers "a judge of the First or Second City Courts of the city of New Orleans, a family court judge, a juvenile court judge, or any district court judge". The order states no reasons, which 9:228(B) permits ("The order need not state the reasons").

### B9. "This order must be attached to the marriage application."
SUPPORTED. 9:228(B): "The written order shall be attached to the marriage application."

### B10. `weddings.md` step 1: "If no birth certificate is on file for you, the law provides a way to ask a court to order the license issued without one; see the Waiver of Birth Certificate form." And `forms.toml` purpose: "Ask the court to order that a marriage license be issued when there is no birth certificate on file."
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. Both omit that the order is available only on competent evidence of birth in a state or territory of the United States (9:228(B)). A foreign-born reader would be misled.
Corrected wording (page): "If you were born in the United States and no birth certificate is on file for you, the law provides a way to ask a court to order the license issued without one; see the Waiver of Birth Certificate form."
Corrected wording (purpose): "Ask the court to order that a marriage license be issued when no birth certificate is on file for an applicant born in the United States."

### Applicants born outside the United States
The form is not wrong, because its opening and its "State or territory of birth" field limit it to United States births. The governing rule for others is R.S. 9:226(C) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107004): "A person born outside of the United States or territory of the United States shall submit a birth certificate under the seal of the United States or shall submit all of the following: (1)(a) A copy of the person's birth certificate under the raised seal or stamp of the vital statistics registration authority of the person's place of birth. (b) If the birth certificate is not printed in English, the party shall submit a translated copy ... (2) A valid and unexpired passport or an unexpired visa accompanied by a Form I-94 issued by the United States, verifying that the applicant is lawfully in the United States." 9:227(A) mentions a letter from a "country", but 9:228 allows the order only on evidence of birth "in any state or territory of the United States". Nothing I found gives a justice of the peace power to waive the birth certificate for a foreign-born applicant. No statement about this is needed on the form beyond the B1 and B10 fixes; do not add advice on what a foreign-born applicant should do.

## C. Judgment of Ownership of a Vehicle (`forms/ownership-judgment.typ`)

Deciding text, C.C.P. art. 4912(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112119):

> "A.(1) A justice of the peace court shall, within its territorial jurisdiction, have jurisdiction, concurrent with the parish or district court, over suits for the possession or ownership of movable property not exceeding five thousand dollars in value ...
> (2) A judgment of ownership of a vehicle ordered by a justice of the peace court shall be recognized by the office of motor vehicles of the Department of Public Safety and Corrections in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 4 of Title 32 of the Louisiana Revised Statutes of 1950."

C.C.P. art. 1201(A) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=111147):

> "A. Citation and service thereof are essential in all civil actions except summary and executory proceedings, divorce actions under Civil Code Article 102, and proceedings under the Children's Code. Without them all proceedings are absolutely null.
> B. The defendant may expressly waive citation and service thereof by any written waiver made part of the record."

C.C.P. art. 4913(B)(9) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112120): "A justice of the peace court has no jurisdiction in any of the following cases or proceedings: ... (9) An in rem or quasi in rem proceeding."

C.C.P. art. 4919(C) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112126): "When the plaintiff has not filed a written petition, the citation shall: ... (2) Describe the movable property and state the value thereof, if the suit is for the ownership or possession of movable property."

C.C.P. art. 4920 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112127): "The defendant shall answer within ten days of the service of citation ..."

C.C.P. art. 4921 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112128): "A. If the defendant fails to answer timely, or if he fails to appear at the trial, and the plaintiff establishes a prima facie case by competent and admissible evidence, a default judgment in favor of the plaintiff may be rendered. B. When the suit is for a sum due on an open account, promissory note, negotiable instrument, or other conventional obligation, prima facie proof may be submitted by affidavit."

C.C.P. art. 4921.1(C)(1) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=322002): "... the justice of the peace or clerk may set the matter for trial upon filing of a petition. The date, time, and location of the trial shall be contained in the citation. The first scheduled trial date shall be not more than forty-five days, nor less than ten days, from the service of the citation. ... If a defendant who has been served with citation fails to appear at the time and place specified in the citation, the judge may enter a default judgment for the plaintiff in the amount proved to be due."

C.C.P. art. 4921.2 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=962202): "A. At trial, it is the duty of the justice of the peace to conduct an informal hearing and to develop all of the facts necessary and relevant to an impartial determination of the case. ... B. The technical rules of evidence are relaxed, and all relevant evidence is admissible, including hearsay, provided the justice of the peace satisfies himself of its general reliability, and further provided that the judgment is founded upon competent evidence."

### V1. "The court can decide who owns a vehicle worth $5,000 or less." (form opening; `small-claims.md` "Ownership of a vehicle"; `forms.toml` purpose "Ask the court to decide who owns a vehicle worth $5,000 or less when the title papers cannot be obtained.")
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. The amount is right: "$5,000 or less" equals art. 4912(A)(1) "not exceeding five thousand dollars in value". The imprecision is in "decide who owns": the article gives jurisdiction over "suits", "within its territorial jurisdiction". A resident reads the sentence as "the court will declare me owner on request".
Corrected wording: "The court can hear a suit over the ownership of a vehicle worth $5,000 or less. C.C.P. art. 4912." The "when the title papers cannot be obtained" clause rests on the Manual and OMV policy only (secondary); it is not in art. 4912. Keep it as a description of the form's use, or omit.

### V2. "The Office of Motor Vehicles sets its own requirements for issuing a title on such a judgment, and a judgment does not guarantee a title. C.C.P. art. 4912; Office of Motor Vehicles Policy 3.00."
SUPPORTED. Art. 4912(A)(2) says recognition is "in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 4 of Title 32". R.S. 32:707(G) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=88520): "The commissioner shall use reasonable diligence in ascertaining whether or not the facts in said application are true ... and, if satisfied that the applicant is the owner of such vehicle and that the application is in the proper form, he shall issue a certificate of title over his signature, but not otherwise." R.S. 32:712 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=88528): "The commissioner, when satisfied of the genuineness of regularity of such transfer, shall issue a certificate of title to the person entitled thereto." "Sets its own requirements" rests on OMV Policy 3.00 (secondary) and R.S. 32:704(C) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=88516): "The Department of Public Safety and Corrections shall promulgate rules and regulations to govern this Chapter".

### V3. `small-claims.md`: "The Office of Motor Vehicles recognizes such a judgment, but it sets its own requirements for issuing a title, and a judgment does not guarantee one."
SUPPORTED. Art. 4912(A)(2): "shall be recognized by the office of motor vehicles"; rest as V2.

### V4. `small-claims.md`: "The Judgment of Ownership of a Vehicle form lists what the court needs."
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. The form lists what OMV Policy 3.00 wants in the judgment. What the court needs under the Code is a suit: a defendant, citation and service (or written waiver), and proof (arts. 1201, 4919 to 4921.2 above). The sentence misleads by omission.
Corrected wording: "The Judgment of Ownership of a Vehicle form asks for the facts the court and the Office of Motor Vehicles need. The owner of record, or the person you acquired the vehicle from, is named as the defendant and is notified before the court decides." Or omit the sentence.

### V5. Heading "Applicant: the person in possession of the vehicle"
UNSUPPORTED. Art. 4912 does not require possession and does not use "applicant"; the party who brings a suit is the plaintiff (arts. 4911(B), 4919, 4921). The possession wording comes from the OMV "Request for Review" sheet in the Manual ("Name of Applicant (owner in possession)"), secondary.
Corrected wording: "Plaintiff (the person asking to be recognized as owner)". If the office wants the fact of possession for OMV, ask it as a question: "Is the vehicle in your possession? Where is it kept?" The location also bears on territorial jurisdiction (O4).

### V6. Attached: "Affidavit of physical inspection by a certified law enforcement officer, if there is no title or registration"
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (secondary source only; no statute found). OMV Policy 3.00: "The physical inspection must be performed by a full-time Peace Officer Standards and Training (P.O.S.T.) certified law enforcement officer who has been certified by the Department of Public Safety and Corrections, Office of State Police, to inspect motor vehicles." The short form could send a resident to the wrong officer.
Corrected wording: "Affidavit of physical inspection, if there is no title or registration. The Office of Motor Vehicles requires the inspection to be made by a full-time P.O.S.T.-certified law enforcement officer certified by the Office of State Police to inspect motor vehicles."

### V7. Attached: "Bill of sale"; "Title or certificate of registration, to verify the vehicle identification number"; "Release of lien, if a lien is recorded"
SUPPORTED, on OMV Policy 3.00 (secondary) and R.S. 32:712: "Such certificate of title shall contain a statement of any chattel mortgages of record in the office of the commissioner, unless such application is accompanied by proper evidence of their satisfaction or extinction." OMV says bill of sale "if available"; the tick box format already makes it conditional.

### V8. Oath: "I swear that the statements in this application are true, and that the vehicle is free of liens unless a release of lien is attached."
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. The first clause is fine. The second makes the applicant swear to a legal status he usually cannot know; liens are a matter of the OMV record (R.S. 32:706(D), https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=88518, security interests "shall be complete and effective as to all persons"). "Unless a release is attached" is also illogical: with a release attached the vehicle is still free of liens.
Corrected wording: "I swear that the statements in this application are true. To my knowledge no lien is recorded against the vehicle, or every recorded lien has been released and the release is attached."

### V9. "Sworn to and subscribed before me on (date)" signed by the Justice
SUPPORTED. R.S. 13:2586.1 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=77373): "A. A justice of the peace shall be an ex officio notary public within his territorial jurisdiction to the extent allowed by this Section. A justice of the peace may exercise the functions of a notary public only to: (1) Administer oaths or affirmations. (2) Notarize sworn statements, affidavits, and acknowledgements." and "E. This Section does not affect the authority of a justice of the peace to receive and execute affidavits, sworn statements, oaths, judicial bonds, or other documents, instruments, or acts that relate to or are involved in matters pending in his court or presented to him in his capacity as a justice of the peace."
Caveat: subsection B(5) says the justice as ex officio notary "has no authority to: ... (5) Draft any documents in the performance of his notary powers as provided in Subsection A of this Section." A court-supplied sworn pleading in a pending suit falls under E. That is one more reason to run this as a docketed suit rather than as a notarial service.

### V10. Second page title "Court Order"
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. The statute's term is "judgment": art. 4912(A)(2) "A judgment of ownership of a vehicle"; C.C.P. art. 4923 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112130): "The judgment shall be in writing and signed by the justice of the peace or the clerk of court." "Court Order" comes from the Attorney General's sample (secondary). OMV accepts "order/judgment".
Corrected wording: "Judgment of Ownership of a Vehicle".

### V11. "Pursuant to Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure Article 4912, I, the undersigned Justice of the Peace, upon receipt of proper documents and the attached affidavit, render judgment as follows."
UNSUPPORTED. This is the Attorney General's sample wording (Manual, about line 10685), not the Code. Art. 4912 grants jurisdiction over "suits". Art. 1201(A) makes citation and service "essential in all civil actions" and says "Without them all proceedings are absolutely null." Art. 4913(B)(9) denies jurisdiction over "An in rem or quasi in rem proceeding", and a judgment of ownership with no defendant is in substance a judgment against the thing. Art. 4921(B) allows affidavit proof on default only "When the suit is for a sum due on an open account, promissory note, negotiable instrument, or other conventional obligation", which an ownership suit is not. A judgment that recites only "receipt of proper documents and the attached affidavit" recites a procedure the Code does not provide and omits the facts that make the judgment valid. The secondary sources agree a proceeding against the owner of record is expected: OMV Policy 3.00, "Adversarial proceedings ... must be against the owner of record. It will be the responsibility of the Justice of the Peace issuing the judgment to provide proof of the proceedings"; Manual line 10563, "Adversarial proceedings (proceedings in which the party seeking relief has given legal notice to the other party and afforded that other party the opportunity to contest the claim) must be against the owner of record."
Corrected wording (each clause maps to an article quoted above):
"This suit for the ownership of a vehicle is before the court under Code of Civil Procedure Article 4912.
Plaintiff: ____. Defendant: ____ (owner of record, or the person from whom the vehicle was acquired).
[ ] The defendant was served with citation on (date) and did not answer or appear. [ ] The defendant appeared. [ ] The defendant waived citation and service in writing, and the waiver is in the record.
After a hearing on (date), on the plaintiff's sworn testimony and the documents filed, the court renders judgment as follows."

### V12. "Items 2 through 4 are according to the sworn affidavit of the applicant."
SUPPORTED. An accurate statement of the source of those findings; it follows the Attorney General's sample ("According to the sworn affidavit of Applicant"). Art. 4921.2(B) allows the evidence "provided the justice of the peace satisfies himself of its general reliability". If V11 is adopted, change "affidavit" to "sworn application and testimony" so the page names the same document throughout.

### V13. Item 1: "The person awarded ownership of the vehicle is"
SUPPORTED on OMV Policy 3.00 ("Name of person awarded ownership of the vehicle"), secondary. Consistent with art. 4912(A)(2).

### V14. Item 2: "The vehicle was acquired on (date)" "for a consideration of $"; application: "Price or other consideration $"
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (minor). OMV: "Consideration (monies, etc.) involved/exchanged"; Manual: "(monies, trade, etc.)". The printed "$" forces a money answer where the vehicle was traded for or donated.
Corrected wording: "for this consideration (money, trade, or other):" with no dollar sign, in both places.

### V15. Item 4: "The vehicle is free of liens."
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. It is pre-printed, so the justice signs it in every case, on the applicant's oath alone. A lienholder is not a party, and under R.S. 32:712 OMV will still note "any chattel mortgages of record ... unless such application is accompanied by proper evidence of their satisfaction or extinction." OMV policy asks that the judgment "state that the vehicle is lien free", and adds that otherwise "a satisfaction of lien is required", so the statement is optional, not mandatory.
Corrected wording: "[ ] According to the plaintiff's sworn statement and the Office of Motor Vehicles record checked on (date), the vehicle is free of liens. [ ] A release of each recorded lien is attached." Tick only what was established. The Manual (line 10605) "strongly encourages" the OMV Request for Review before judgment to confirm liens, brands and stolen flags.

### V16. Item 7: "The value of the vehicle is less than $5,000. Facts that support that value, if a valuation guide gives a higher one:"
OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE. Art. 4912(A)(1) sets the limit at "not exceeding five thousand dollars in value"; the form's own first sentence says "$5,000 or less". "Less than $5,000" is OMV's phrase ("The value of the vehicle shall be less than $5,000"), secondary. It is also a pre-printed finding with no figure. Art. 4911(B) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112118): "the amount in dispute is determined by the amount demanded or value asserted in good faith by the plaintiff".
Corrected wording: "The value of the vehicle is $____, which does not exceed $5,000. If the J.D. Power retail value is higher, the facts that support the lower value are:" Allow more than one line; OMV asks for "a detailed statement of facts".

### V17. "Judgment is rendered awarding ownership of the vehicle to the applicant, ____" with date and signature
SUPPORTED as wording, subject to V11. Art. 4912(A)(2); art. 4923 (writing, signed). If V5 is adopted, "plaintiff" replaces "applicant".

### V18. Items 3, 5, 6 (seller and last known address; year, make, model, VIN; nature and history of the acquisition and why title papers are unavailable)
SUPPORTED on OMV Policy 3.00's list (secondary). No statute prescribes these contents.

### V19. `forms.toml` title "Judgment of Ownership of a Vehicle"
SUPPORTED. Art. 4912(A)(2) uses these words.

### OMV Policy 3.00 checklist against the judgment page
Present: name of person awarded ownership (1); date acquired (2); seller (3); nature/history and why documents are unavailable (6); year, make, model, VIN (5); consideration (2); lien-free statement (4); value statement (7); letterhead; signature and date; case number.
Absent: the owner of record, and "proof of the proceedings" against that person. See O1.

### Title 32
No contradiction found in the sections read (R.S. 32:702, 704, 705 (first 8,000 characters), 706, 706.1, 707, 712, 735). Points that bear on the form:
- R.S. 32:706(A): "no person buying a vehicle from the owner thereof ... shall acquire a marketable title in or to the vehicle until the purchaser shall have obtained a certificate of title to the vehicle". The form's "a judgment does not guarantee a title" is consistent; nothing on the form should say the judgment gives a title.
- R.S. 32:707(A): purchaser "shall file an application for a new certificate of title within five days". Not contradicted.
- R.S. 32:707(M): OMV itself may title a vehicle 25 years old or older, abandoned three years, without a bill of sale. An alternative route; no need to mention it.
- R.S. 32:735 concerns auto title companies and has no bearing on the form's text.

## D. Omissions (no verdict; things the forms leave out)

### O1. Vehicle judgment: no defendant, no owner of record, no citation, no hearing
The most serious finding. See V11. The application asks only "Owner of record, if different and if known", and the judgment page does not mention that person at all. As written, the justice would sign a judgment in a proceeding with one party. Recommended: (a) caption the application as plaintiff versus defendant with a case number, the defendant being the owner of record, or the seller when there is no record owner; (b) make "Owner of record and last known address" a required field; (c) issue citation under art. 4919, which for an oral or form claim must "Describe the movable property and state the value thereof"; (d) recite service, appearance or default, and the hearing date in the judgment (wording at V11); (e) keep the return of service in the file as OMV's "proof of the proceedings". If the owner of record is unknown or cannot be served, I could not verify a procedure open to this court; the Manual (line 10524) says of vehicles with no proof of ownership, "Must obtain judgment from a district court". Until that is settled, the form should not promise a judgment in that case.

### O2. Birth certificate order: no basis for "complied with all other requirements"
The order makes the finding 9:228(B) requires, but the application collects nothing about the other license requirements (R.S. 9:224 sworn application, 9:225 other party's birth certificate and both parties' identification). Suggested addition to the application, under "Attached" or as a note: "Bring to the hearing the marriage license application and the other documents the clerk of court requires for both of you." This is a process point; the order wording is correct.

### O3. Birth certificate order: where to apply afterward
The order runs only to "an issuing official within the parish where his court is situated". `weddings.md` says "A license from any Louisiana parish is good". A resident holding this order must apply in St. Tammany Parish. Suggested line under the applicant's signature: "The order is directed to the issuing official in St. Tammany Parish."

### O4. Vehicle: territorial jurisdiction and venue
Art. 4912(A)(1) limits jurisdiction to "within its territorial jurisdiction"; art. 4916 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112123) applies the general venue rules with "parish" read as "the territorial jurisdiction of the justice of the peace court". The form does not ask where the defendant lives or where the vehicle is kept. Add both fields.

### O5. Vehicle: cases OMV will refuse
OMV Policy 3.00 lists cases where it "will not accept court orders": a vehicle bought from a dealer still in business; damaged vehicles without disclosure or affidavit of repairs; branded vehicles (auto hulk, crushed, dismantled, stolen, certificate of destruction). The form's "does not guarantee a title" covers this in general terms. A short list on the form would spare residents a wasted fee, but it rests on policy only; optional. The Manual (line 10621) also says the title "will be branded 'Justice of the Peace Court Order'"; secondary, optional, and I could not confirm it against Policy 3.00's extract.

### O6. Vehicle: notice of judgment
C.C.P. art. 4922 (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112129): "Notice of the signing of any final judgment shall be given in accordance with Article 1913." Art. 1913(C) (https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=111339): "notice of the signing of a default judgment shall be mailed by the clerk of court to the defendant at the address where personal service was obtained or to the last known address of the defendant." A court-use line for the date notice was mailed would complete the record. Not a statement on the form; an office step.

### O7. Delay waiver: place of ceremony
The certificate recites authority to perform the marriage (D5) but the form does not record where the ceremony will be. Authority is territorial (9:203(A)(5)). Either drop the recital or add a "Place of ceremony (parish)" field.

## E. Legal advice and misleading-by-omission check

- None of the three forms tells a resident what to do in the resident's own case. The opening summaries state the law; that is information, not advice.
- Misleading by omission: V1 and V4 (vehicle: implies a one-party request suffices), B1 and B10 (birth certificate: conditions and United States birth limit), V6 (which officer may inspect), O3 (order good only in St. Tammany).
- The footer "Court staff cannot give legal advice" is appropriate. `small-claims.md` "The Office of Motor Vehicles recognizes such a judgment" should stay paired with "does not guarantee one", as it is.
