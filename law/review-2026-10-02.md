Reviewed 2026-10-02. Statute text was fetched from legis.la.gov on that date and is quoted as fetched. Secondary sources are labelled as secondary. The site's own source notes (forms/SOURCES.md) were deliberately not read.

Link check: every legis.la.gov link on the site (43 distinct `d=` numbers across the four service pages and the About page) opens the article or section the site says it is. No link is mislabelled. Each was fetched through the printable page with the same `d=` number.

The statement the justice named, "No written pleading is required", is correct. It is the text of C.C.P. art. 4917(A) (entry 8).

The most consequential finding is the five-day count (entries 30 and 74). The site says "Do not count legal holidays" but does not say that Saturdays and Sundays are legal holidays for this purpose (R.S. 1:55(E)(3)). A landlord who reads the site will count the weekend and file too early.

URL convention below: `L(n)` means `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=n`.

## Part 1. Small claims page (`src/content/services/small-claims.md`)

### 1. Amount and subject
> "The court decides disputes over money or movable property worth $5,000 or less."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4911(A), L(112118): "The civil jurisdiction of a justice of the peace court is concurrent with the district court in cases where the amount in dispute does not exceed five thousand dollars." C.C.P. art. 4912(A)(1), L(112119): "...jurisdiction, concurrent with the parish or district court, over suits for the possession or ownership of movable property not exceeding five thousand dollars in value..."

### 2. No lawyer required
> "You may speak for yourself; no lawyer is required."

**SUPPORTED**, with a limit the site does not state. R.S. 37:212(B), L(93560): "Nothing in this Section prohibits any person from attending to and caring for his own business, claims, or demands". For a business, R.S. 37:212(C): "Nothing in this Section shall prohibit any partnership, corporation, or other legal entity from asserting or defending any claim, not exceeding five thousand dollars, on its own behalf in the courts of limited jurisdiction or on its own behalf through a duly authorized partner, shareholder, officer, employee, or duly authorized agent or representative. No partnership, corporation, or other entity may assert any claim on behalf of another entity or any claim assigned to it."
Note: a business suing on a claim that was assigned to it (a debt buyer, a collection agency) is outside the exception. The forms say "Signature of plaintiff or representative" without this limit. Not wrong, but see Part 8.

### 3. Fees and the fee note
> "Filing a new suit, one defendant $130", "Each additional defendant $20", "State law sets the most the court may charge for each. R.S. 13:2590."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2590(A), L(77379): "A justice of the peace may demand and receive up to the following amounts in all civil matters: (1) New suit: one hundred thirty dollars, and twenty dollars per additional defendant. (2) Eviction proceeding: one hundred twenty dollars, and twenty dollars per additional defendant." The fees charged equal the ceilings; none exceeds one.
Secondary: the Manual (p. 49) shows $100 for both. The Manual is out of date here (statute amended by Acts 2023, No. 61).

### 4. The $5,000 limit and what does not count
> "The amount in dispute is $5,000 or less. Interest, court costs, attorney fees, and penalties do not count toward the limit."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4911(B): "the amount in dispute is determined by the amount demanded or value asserted in good faith by the plaintiff, but does not include interest, court costs, attorney fees, or penalties, whether provided by agreement or by law."

### 5. Movable property
> "Or you are asking for possession or ownership of movable property (things other than land and buildings, such as a vehicle, tools, or furniture) worth $5,000 or less."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4912(A)(1), quoted in entry 1. The parenthesis is a lay gloss, not statute text; it is accurate for the examples given.

### 6. Venue
> "The case belongs in Ward 10. The general rule is that a person is sued where that person lives, and a Louisiana company where its registered office is. Other rules apply to some cases. If you are not sure, ask the office before you file."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE.**
C.C.P. art. 42, L(111929): "(1) An individual who is domiciled in the state shall be brought in the parish of his domicile; or if he resides but is not domiciled in the state, in the parish of his residence. (2) A domestic corporation, a domestic insurer, or a domestic limited liability company shall be brought in the parish where its registered office is located. (3) A domestic partnership, or a domestic unincorporated association, shall be brought in the parish where its principal business establishment is located."
C.C.P. art. 4916, L(112123): "The rules of venue provided in Articles 41 through 45 and in Articles 71 through 79 apply to suits brought in justice of the peace courts, except where these articles use the word 'parish' it shall be construed to mean the territorial jurisdiction of the justice of the peace court."
What is imprecise:
- "Company" is wider than the statute. The registered-office rule covers corporations, insurers, and limited liability companies. A partnership or unincorporated association is sued where its principal business establishment is.
- "Lives" stands in for "domicile". Acceptable as plain language, but a person can live in more than one place.
- "If you are not sure, ask the office" invites staff to decide a legal question that the court may later have to rule on if the defendant objects (see Part 7).
Recommendation: "The general rule is that a person is sued in the ward of that person's domicile (permanent home), and a Louisiana corporation or limited liability company in the ward of its registered office. Other rules apply to some cases (C.C.P. arts. 42 and 4916)." Drop "ask the office before you file", or change it to "ask a lawyer".
Secondary: Manual p. 57 uses "where the defendant lives" for individuals and lists partnerships separately.

### 7. Cases the court cannot hear
> "a case about who owns land or a building; divorce, custody, visitation, child support, or spousal support; a succession (the estate of a person who has died); a case in which the state, a parish, a city, or another public body is the defendant; a claim of civil or political rights under the state or federal constitution. The full list is in C.C.P. art. 4913."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4913(B), L(112120): "(1) A case involving title to immovable property. ... (3) A case in which the plaintiff asserts civil or political rights under the federal or state constitutions. (4) A claim for annulment of marriage, separation from bed and board, divorce, separation of property, custody, visitation, spousal support, or child support. (5) A succession, interdiction, receivership, liquidation, habeas corpus, or quo warranto proceeding. (6) A case in which the state, or a parish, municipal, or other political corporation is a defendant." The site says "among others", so the partial list is honest.
Note: R.S. 13:2586(B), L(77372), adds "or when a succession is a defendant".

### 8. Oral claim. "No written pleading is required."
> "You may tell the court your claim in person or put it in writing. No written pleading is required."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4917, L(112124), in full:
"A. A party or his attorney may state the claim, exceptions, defenses, or other pleas orally to the justice of the peace or the clerk of court. No written pleadings shall be required.
B. A party may file written pleadings if he so desires, but additional fees resulting from written pleadings not required shall not be imposed upon the party cast as costs of court.
C. A defendant shall include in his answer, whether oral or in writing, all of the exceptions upon which he intends to rely."
History line: "Acts 1986, No. 156, §1." No later amendment.
Secondary: Manual p. 64 and p. 84: "This claim may be filed orally or in writing with the justice of the peace. No written pleadings are required, but they are permitted and preferred." AG Guide (April 2024) p. 3 says the same.
Limits worth knowing (the sentence is still right where it stands): some papers must be written. The notice to vacate (art. 4701), the application to proceed without costs (art. 5183, affidavits), a demand for a trial date under art. 4921.1(A) ("written demand"), and an appeal (a suit filed in district court, art. 4925).

### 9. What the Statement of Claim records
> "The Statement of Claim form asks for everything the court must record: who the parties are, where they can be found, and what you claim and why."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4918, L(112125): "When no written pleadings are required, the justice of the peace or the clerk of court shall record in a permanent book or case file the title of the case, the docket number, the name and address of all parties, a brief statement of the nature and amount of the claim..." C.C.P. art. 4919(C), L(112126), adds that the citation shall "State the amount and nature of the claim and the year or years in which the indebtedness was contracted or arose" and describe any note and any movable property with its value. The form has fields for each. Optional tightening: "asks for what the court must record at filing".

### 10. Costs in advance and the pauper articles
> "The court may ask for costs in advance. If you cannot afford them, the law lets you ask to go forward without paying in advance (C.C.P. arts. 5181 through 5188). Ask the office how."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2590(D): "Except when the plaintiff is relieved from the necessity of paying costs or furnishing security therefor, under Code of Civil Procedure Articles 5181 through 5188 or R.S. 13:4521, a justice of the peace may demand that the plaintiff provide costs in advance." C.C.P. art. 5181(A), L(112172): "an individual who is unable to pay the costs of court because of poverty and lack of means may prosecute or defend a judicial proceeding ... in any trial or appellate court without paying the costs in advance or as they accrue or furnishing security therefor."
Not stated on the site (see Part 8): the privilege is for an "individual" only; art. 5183, L(112174), requires the applicant's affidavit and "The affidavit of a third person other than the applicant's attorney"; and art. 5188, L(112179): "if judgment is rendered against a party who has been permitted to litigate without the payment of costs, he shall be condemned to pay the costs incurred by him".

### 11. Citation and service
> "The court issues a citation. The constable serves it, or the court sends it by certified mail."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4919(A): "The citation must be signed by the justice of the peace or the clerk of court issuing it". Art. 4919(D)(1): "Service of citation or other process may be made by the court by certified mail, with return receipt requested, when costs therefore are posted with the court." R.S. 13:2586(F): "A constable of the justice of the peace court is authorized to effectuate service of process within the parish of that court for any case before that court."

### 12. Trial date in the citation
> "If the citation gives a trial date, the defendant must come to court on that date. The first trial date is at least 10 days and not more than 45 days after the citation is served."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4921.1(C)(1), L(322002): "the justice of the peace or clerk may set the matter for trial upon filing of a petition. The date, time, and location of the trial shall be contained in the citation. The first scheduled trial date shall be not more than forty-five days, nor less than ten days, from the service of the citation. If the defendant appears, he need not file an answer unless ordered to do so by the court." Art. 4919(A)(5)(b): the person cited "must either comply with the demand of the plaintiff against him or appear in the court issuing the citation at the time and date provided".

### 13. Ten days to answer; oral or written answer
> "If the citation gives no trial date, the defendant must answer within 10 days of being served. The answer may be spoken or written."

**SUPPORTED**, with one exception not stated. C.C.P. art. 4920, L(112127): "The defendant shall answer within ten days of the service of citation, except that, when the citation is served through the secretary of state, the delay, as to all defendants, shall be fifteen days after service." Oral answer: art. 4917(A), (C).
The exception only lengthens the time, and the page tells the defendant to read the citation for the deadline, so no one is harmed. Optional: "within 10 days of being served (15 days if served through the Secretary of State)".

### 14. The trial
> "The hearing is informal. The judge asks questions to find the facts, and may help the parties reach a settlement. The strict rules of evidence are relaxed, but the judgment must rest on reliable evidence. Bring your witnesses and your papers."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4921.2, L(962202): "A. At trial, it is the duty of the justice of the peace to conduct an informal hearing and to develop all of the facts necessary and relevant to an impartial determination of the case. ... He may attempt to mediate disputes and encourage fair settlements among the parties. B. The technical rules of evidence are relaxed, and all relevant evidence is admissible, including hearsay, provided the justice of the peace satisfies himself of its general reliability, and further provided that the judgment is founded upon competent evidence."
Optional: the statute's word is "competent", not "reliable".

### 15. Judgment, default, dismissal
> "The judgment is in writing and signed. If a defendant who was served does not answer or appear, the court may give a default judgment for what the plaintiff proves is owed. If the plaintiff does not appear at a trial date set in the citation, the court may dismiss the case."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4923, L(112130): "The judgment shall be in writing and signed by the justice of the peace or the clerk of court." C.C.P. art. 4921(A), L(112128): "If the defendant fails to answer timely, or if he fails to appear at the trial, and the plaintiff establishes a prima facie case by competent and admissible evidence, a default judgment in favor of the plaintiff may be rendered." Art. 4921.1(C)(1): "If a defendant who has been served with citation fails to appear at the time and place specified in the citation, the judge may enter a default judgment for the plaintiff in the amount proved to be due. If the plaintiff does not appear, the judge may enter an order dismissing the action without prejudice."

### 16. A judgment does not collect
> "A judgment says who owes what. It does not collect the money. If the other party does not pay, ask the office what steps the court can take."

**SUPPORTED** (no statute contradicts it; enforcement is by separate writs, R.S. 13:2586(E)(1) and R.S. 13:2590(A)(3), (9), (10), (20)). Secondary: AG Guide p. 14: "The court will not collect the money for you."

### 17. If you have been sued: deadlines
> "Do not ignore the citation. Read it for your deadline or trial date. If it gives a trial date, come to court on that date. If it does not, answer within 10 days of the day you were served. You may use the Defendant's Answer form or answer in person."

**SUPPORTED.** Arts. 4919(A)(5), 4920, 4921.1(C), 4917, quoted above.

### 18. What the answer must contain
> "Your answer must include every objection and defense you intend to rely on."

**OVERSTATED.** Art. 4917(C): "A defendant shall include in his answer, whether oral or in writing, all of the exceptions upon which he intends to rely." The statute requires the exceptions (objections such as wrong venue, bad service, prescription). It does not say that every defense on the merits must be in the answer. "Must include every ... defense" tells a defendant that an unlisted defense is lost; the article does not say that.
Secondary: the Manual's instruction sheet (p. 88) and the AG Guide (p. 5) say the answer "should" contain every defense. That is advice, not the statute.
Recommendation: "Your answer must include every objection to the suit itself that you intend to rely on (the law calls these exceptions). C.C.P. art. 4917." Or omit.

### 19. Doing nothing
> "If you do nothing, the court may give judgment against you."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4921(A); art. 4921.1(C)(1).

### 20. Who may appeal
> "Either party may appeal."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE.** Arts. 4924 and 4925 speak of "the appellant" and do not say either party may always appeal. C.C.P. art. 2085, L(111378): "An appeal cannot be taken by a party who confessed judgment in the proceedings in the trial court or who voluntarily and unconditionally acquiesced in a judgment rendered against him." The Answer form offers a consent to judgment (entry 68). I could not confirm from the text alone that art. 2085 governs the trial de novo from a justice court, so the plain statement is not safe.
Recommendation: "A party who loses may appeal." (Secondary: AG Guide p. 14 uses "A party who loses their case ... may appeal".)

### 21. What an appeal is and where it goes
> "An appeal is a new trial in the parish court or, where there is no parish court, in the district court of the parish."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4924, L(112131): "A. Appeal from a judgment rendered by a justice of the peace court or a clerk of court shall be taken to the parish court or, if there is no parish court, to the district court of the parish in which the justice of the peace court is situated. B. The case is tried de novo on appeal. ... C. No further appeal from the judgment of the parish or district court is allowed."

### 22. Fifteen days
> "You must file it within 15 days of the date of the judgment, or of the day you were served with notice of the judgment when notice is required."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4925(A), L(112132): "The appellant ... shall file suit for a trial de novo in the district court or the parish court within fifteen days from the date of the judgment or from the service of notice of judgment, when such notice is necessary."
Notes: (a) art. 4925(B): "When an application for new trial is timely filed, however, the delay for appeal commences on the day after the motion is denied". (b) C.C.P. art. 4922, L(112129), now reads: "Notice of the signing of any final judgment shall be given in accordance with Article 1913", last amended by "Acts 2026, No. 127, §1". The Manual's account of art. 4922 (p. 102) predates that change. The site's wording tracks art. 4925 and stays correct.

### 23. Copies of the appeal
> "You must also give a copy to the other parties and to this court."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4925(A): "A copy of the suit for trial de novo shall be provided to all parties or their attorney of record. A copy of either the notice of suit for trial de novo or the suit pleadings shall also be provided to the justice of the peace court whose judgment is being appealed."

### 24. Where a Ward 10 appeal is filed
> "For Ward 10, the appeal is filed with the St. Tammany Parish Clerk of Court, 701 North Columbia Street, Covington, for the 22nd Judicial District Court."

**SUPPORTED**, with two things not checked against primary law. R.S. 13:477(22), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=77849`: "The parishes of Washington and St. Tammany shall compose the Twenty-Second District." The address matches the Clerk of Court's own contact page (secondary: sttammanyclerk.org). I did not fetch a statute that proves St. Tammany has no parish court; I know of none.

## Part 2. Evictions page (`src/content/services/evictions.md`)

### 25. Which evictions the court can hear
> "The court can hear an eviction when the property is in Ward 10 and it is: a home, whatever the rent; or a business property or farmland with rent of $5,000 a month or less."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4912(A)(1): "within its territorial jurisdiction ... over suits by landowners or lessors for the eviction of occupants or tenants of leased residential premises, regardless of the amount of monthly or yearly rent". Art. 4912(B): "leased commercial premises and leased farmlands where the amount of the monthly rental does not exceed five thousand dollars per month". Fee: $120 and $20, equal to the ceiling in R.S. 13:2590(A)(2).

### 26. Evictions the court cannot hear
> "The court cannot hear the case if the dispute is about who owns the property, or if the defendant is the state, a parish, a city, or another public body."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4913(B)(1), (6), quoted in entry 7.

### 27. Written notice, five days, by the landlord or agent
> "The landlord, or the landlord's agent, must deliver a written notice that gives the tenant at least five days to leave."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4701, L(112073): "the lessor or his agent shall cause written notice to vacate the premises to be delivered to the lessee. The notice shall allow the lessee not less than five days from the date of its delivery to vacate the leased premises."

### 28. The court does not deliver the notice
> "The court does not deliver this notice for you."

**SUPPORTED** as office practice. Secondary: Manual p. 152: "the justice of the peace cannot deliver the written notice to vacate (or notice of termination) to the tenant", and a constable who delivers it "is then disqualified to act in his official capacity as constable in any subsequently filed eviction proceeding" (citing La. Atty. Gen. Op. No. 12-0025).

### 29. Occupants
> "An owner who wants an occupant who is not a tenant to leave gives the same five days' notice."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4702, L(112074): "When an owner of immovable property wishes to evict the occupant therefrom, after the purpose of the occupancy has ceased, the owner or his agent, shall first cause a written notice to vacate the property to be delivered to the occupant. This notice shall allow the occupant five days from its delivery to vacate the premises."

### 30. Counting the five days
> "Count the five days correctly. Do not count the day the notice is delivered. Do not count legal holidays. If you are unsure whether a day counts, ask the office."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (material omission).** Each sentence is right, but the passage leaves out that Saturdays and Sundays are legal holidays for this count.
C.C.P. art. 5059, L(112147): "A. In computing a period of time allowed or prescribed by law or by order of court, the date of the act, event, or default from which the period begins to run is not included. The last day of the period is included, unless it is a legal holiday, in which event the period runs until the end of the next day that is not a legal holiday. ... C. A half-holiday is considered a legal holiday. A legal holiday is included in the computation of a period of time allowed or prescribed, except when: (1) It is expressly excluded. (2) It would otherwise be the last day of the period. (3) The period is less than seven days."
R.S. 1:55(E)(3), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=74097`: "Only the enumerated holidays in Paragraph (1) of this Subsection, days of closure under Paragraph (2) of this Subsection, Mardi Gras only in those parishes in which the governing authority of the parish declares a holiday under authority of Paragraph (A)(3) of this Section, and all Saturdays and Sundays shall be considered as legal holidays for the purposes of Code of Civil Procedure Article 5059."
Secondary: Manual p. 151: "The five (5) day period does not include weekends or holidays and does not include date of service." Manual p. 64: "(Saturdays and Sundays are considered legal holidays)."
A reader who is not a lawyer will not treat a Saturday as a "legal holiday". A petition filed on the fifth calendar day is premature, which the Manual lists as a tenant's defense (p. 155: "Petition of Eviction was filed prior to expiration of Notice to Vacate").
Remaining uncertainty: R.S. 1:55(E)(3) sits in a subsection about clerks of district, parish, and city courts. That it makes weekends legal holidays for art. 5059 is plain. Which weekday holidays count in a justice court is less plain, and the list in (E)(1) differs from the list in (A)(1).
Recommendation: "Do not count the day the notice is delivered. Do not count Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. C.C.P. art. 5059; R.S. 1:55." Add R.S. 1:55 to the list of statutes. Reconsider "ask the office" (Part 7).

### 31. The petition
> "File a petition of eviction. If the tenant has not left when the notice period ends, call the office for an appointment and file the petition with this court. It must state the reason for the eviction."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4731(A), L(112078): "If the lessee or occupant fails to comply with the notice to vacate required under this Title, or if the lessee has waived his right to notice ... the lessor or owner, or agent thereof, may cause the lessee or occupant to be cited summarily by a court of competent jurisdiction to show cause why he should not be ordered to deliver possession of the premises to the lessor or owner. The rule to show cause shall state the grounds upon which eviction is sought."

### 32. The hearing date
> "The court orders the tenant to come to court and show why the eviction should not be ordered. The constable serves the order. The hearing is no earlier than the third day after service."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4732(A), L(112079): "The court shall make the rule returnable not earlier than the third day after service thereof, at which time the court shall try the rule and hear any defense which is made."
Secondary: Manual p. 154 counts these three days without the day of service, weekends, or holidays. The site quotes the statute and does not count for the reader, which is the safe course.

### 33. Judgment at the hearing
> "Both sides may speak and show their evidence. If the court finds for the landlord, or if the tenant does not come, the court gives a judgment of eviction at once."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4732(B): "If the court finds the lessor or owner entitled to the relief sought, or if the lessee or occupant fails to answer or to appear at the trial, the court shall render immediately a judgment of eviction ordering the lessee or occupant to deliver possession of the premises to the lessor or owner."

### 34. Twenty-four hours, warrant, two witnesses
> "The tenant has 24 hours to leave. If the tenant does not, the court issues a warrant and the constable delivers the property to the landlord, in the presence of two witnesses."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4733, L(112080): "If the lessee or occupant does not comply with the judgment of eviction within twenty-four hours after its rendition, the court shall issue immediately a warrant directed to and commanding its sheriff, constable, or marshal to deliver possession of the premises to the lessor or owner." C.C.P. art. 4734, L(112081): "The sheriff, constable, or marshal shall execute a warrant rendered under Article 4733 in the presence of two witnesses, by clearing the premises of any property therein".
Optional: "within 24 hours after the judgment is given" is closer to the text. I could not determine whether weekend or holiday hours count; the statute says "twenty-four hours" and art. 5059 speaks of days.
Secondary: Manual p. 155 states it the same way as the site.

### 35. Waiver of notice in the lease
> "If a written lease contains the tenant's waiver of the notice to vacate, the landlord may file the petition as soon as the tenant's right to stay has ended."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4701, third paragraph: "A lessee may waive the notice requirements of this Article by written waiver contained in the lease, in which case, upon termination of the lessee's right of occupancy for any reason, the lessor or his agent may immediately institute eviction proceedings".

### 36. Lease with no fixed end; month-to-month
> "The lease has no fixed end date. The notice the law requires to end the lease also serves as the notice to vacate. For a month-to-month lease, that notice must be given at least ten calendar days before the end of the month."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4701, second paragraph: "If the lease has no definite term, the notice required by law for its termination shall be considered as a notice to vacate under this Article." C.C. art. 2728, L(109792): "(2) In a month-to-month lease, ten calendar days before the end of that month".
Two notes. (a) The termination notice must be written: C.C. art. 2729, `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=109793`: "If the leased thing is an immovable or is a movable used as residence, the notice of termination shall be in writing." The site does not say so here. (b) The bullet does not say when the ten-day notice is the one that applies. The Manual (p. 152, secondary) limits it: "If the landlord does not have a reason to evict but wants to regain possession of the premises, he must give the tenant written notice of termination". The statute text does not draw that line in words, so I do not recommend adding it. I recommend adding "in writing" and the citation to C.C. arts. 2727 to 2729, and see entry 71 on the Notice to Vacate form.

### 37. Notice on the door
> "The tenant cannot be found. If the property is abandoned or closed, or no one knows where the tenant is, the notice may be attached to a door of the property."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4703, L(112075): "If the premises are abandoned or closed, or if the whereabouts of the lessee or occupant is unknown, all notices, process, pleadings, and orders required to be delivered or served on the lessee or occupant under this Title may be attached to a door of the premises".

### 38. Tenant must come
> "Come to the hearing. If you do not come, the court must give judgment against you."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4732(B), "shall render immediately", quoted in entry 33.

### 39. Sworn answer
> "Tell the court your defense. To keep the right to stay in the property during an appeal, your answer must be made under oath and must state a defense that entitles you to stay."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C.P. art. 4735, L(112082): "An appeal does not suspend execution of a judgment of eviction unless the defendant has answered the rule under oath, pleading an affirmative defense entitling him to retain possession of the premises". See Part 7 on tone.

### 40. Appeal and bond
> "An appeal does not stop the eviction unless you gave that sworn answer, and you ask for the appeal and file the appeal bond within 24 hours of the judgment. The court sets the amount of the bond."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4735, continued: "...and the appeal has been applied for and the appeal bond filed within twenty-four hours after the rendition of the judgment of eviction. The amount of the suspensive appeal bond shall be determined by the court in an amount sufficient to protect the appellee against all such damage as he may sustain as a result of the appeal."

## Part 3. Weddings page (`src/content/services/weddings.md`)

### 41. Authority; license and witnesses
> "The Justice of the Peace performs civil marriage ceremonies. You bring a Louisiana marriage license and two adult witnesses."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:202, L(106877): "A marriage ceremony may be performed by: ... (2) A state judge or justice of the peace." R.S. 9:205, L(106896); R.S. 9:244, L(107105), quoted below.

### 42. Where a justice of the peace may officiate
> "A justice of the peace may perform ceremonies in the parish where the court sits and in the other parishes of the same Supreme Court district."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:203(A)(5), L(106887): "A justice of the peace within the parish in which the court of that justice of the peace is situated, and in any parish within the same supreme court district, or in a parish that has no justice of the peace court, except for Orleans Parish."
For reference, R.S. 13:101.2(1), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=1387456`: "District 1 is composed of Livingston Parish; Precincts 9-41C, 9-41D, 9-45, and 9-45A of Orleans Parish; St. Bernard Parish; St. Tammany Parish; Tangipahoa Parish; and Washington Parish." How "except for Orleans Parish" applies to a district that includes part of Orleans is unclear. The site names no parishes and holds ceremonies at the office, so the question does not arise.

### 43. Who issues the license
> "The court does not issue marriage licenses. The clerk of court does."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:221(A), L(106990): "(2) The clerk of court, in any other parish" (other than Orleans).

### 44. License from any parish
> "Apply to a clerk of court. A license from any Louisiana parish is good for a ceremony anywhere in the state."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:222, L(106991): "A marriage license may be issued in any parish, regardless of where the ceremony is to be performed or the parties reside."

### 45. The application and its documents
> "Each of you signs the application under oath. The clerk will ask for a certified copy of each birth certificate, identification such as a driver's license or passport, and your Social Security numbers. Ask the clerk about its fee and about anything you do not have."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:224(A), L(106999): "shall be sworn to and signed by both parties before a notary public, deputy clerk, or deputy registrar" and "(6) Each party's social security number, if both parties were born in any state or territory of the United States or are naturalized citizens". R.S. 9:225(A), L(107001): "(1)(a) A certified copy of each party's birth certificate ... (4) A valid and unexpired driver's license, a government issued identification card, or a valid and unexpired passport". Exceptions exist (armed forces, court order, no Social Security number); "ask the clerk" covers them.

### 46. The 24-hour wait and its waiver
> "The ceremony cannot take place until 24 hours after the license is issued. For serious reasons, the Justice of the Peace may waive the wait. Ask the office."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:241, L(107088): "An officiant may not perform a marriage ceremony until twenty-four hours have elapsed since the issuance of the marriage license." R.S. 9:242(A), L(107089): "A judge, justice of the peace, or retired justice of the peace authorized to perform the marriage may waive the twenty-four-hour delay upon application of the parties giving serious and meritorious reasons. His certificate authorizing the immediate performance of the ceremony must be attached to the marriage license."

### 47. Thirty days
> "The license expires 30 days after it is issued."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:235, L(107076): "A marriage license is valid for thirty days from the date of issuance. No officiant shall perform a marriage after the license has expired."

### 48. Presence, witnesses, license
> "Both of you must be present in person, with two witnesses of full age. Bring the license. The ceremony cannot be performed without it."

**SUPPORTED.** C.C. art. 91, L(111058): "The parties must be physically present at the ceremony when it is performed." R.S. 9:244: "The marriage ceremony shall be performed in the presence of two competent witnesses of full age." R.S. 9:205: "An officiant may not perform a marriage ceremony until he has received a license authorizing him to perform that marriage ceremony."

### 49. The certificate
> "You, your witnesses, and the Justice of the Peace sign the marriage certificate. You receive one copy. The Justice of the Peace files the other copies with the clerk of court who issued the license, within ten days."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:245(A)(1), L(107116): "signed by the parties to the marriage and by the witnesses, and the signature and title of the officiant". R.S. 9:253(A), L(107123): "The officiant shall give one copy of the marriage certificate to the married parties. Within ten days after the ceremony, he shall file the other two copies of the certificate of marriage with the clerk of court who issued the marriage license."

### 50. Under 18
> "If either of you is under 18, other rules apply. Ask the clerk of court before you apply."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:221(B): "No marriage license for a minor under the age of sixteen shall be issued. No marriage license for a minor of the age of sixteen or seventeen shall be issued where there is an age difference of three years or greater between the persons seeking the marriage license." Ch.C. art. 1545, `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=73003`: "An officiant may not perform a marriage ceremony in which a minor sixteen or seventeen is a party unless the minor has judicial authorization and the written consent to marry of either: (1) Both of his parents. (2) The tutor of his person. (3) A person who has been awarded custody of the minor."
The Manual's account of minors (p. 186) predates Acts 2019, No. 401 and is out of date. The site's general remark is safe.

### 51. Ceremony fee
> "Ceremony on a weekday $150", "Ceremony on a Saturday or Sunday $200"

**SUPPORTED** (no ceiling). R.S. 13:2588, L(77377): "A justice of the peace who performs a marriage ceremony may charge a usual and customary fee for that service."

## Part 4. Notary page (`src/content/services/notary.md`)

### 52. Ex officio notary, limited, within the territory
> "A justice of the peace is a notary by virtue of the office, with limited powers, and only within the court's own territory."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2586.1(A), L(77373): "A justice of the peace shall be an ex officio notary public within his territorial jurisdiction to the extent allowed by this Section."
Note: R.S. 13:2586.1(C): "This Section does not prevent a justice of the peace from qualifying to be a notary public with full authority pursuant to R.S. 35:1 et seq." If the justice also holds a regular commission, the page understates her powers. That is an office fact.

### 53. What the justice can notarize
> "administer oaths and affirmations; notarize sworn statements, affidavits, and acknowledgments; notarize bills of sale, transfers, donations, chattel mortgages, and other papers that affect movable property or its title, including vehicle title papers filed with the Office of Motor Vehicles; make protests."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2586.1(A)(1) to (4): "(1) Administer oaths or affirmations. (2) Notarize sworn statements, affidavits, and acknowledgements. (3) Make protests. (4) Notarize bills of sale, transfers, conveyances, exchanges, donations, chattel mortgages, and other documents or instruments affecting movable property or the title thereto, and to notarize title documents relating to movable property when such title documents are required by law, including those title documents required to be registered with the office of motor vehicles".

### 54. What the justice cannot do
> "notarize any paper that sells, transfers, or mortgages land or buildings; notarize or prepare a will or a prenuptial agreement; prepare inventories, appraisements, or partitions; write your document for you."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (incomplete).** R.S. 13:2586.1(B): "(1) Receive or notarize any document effecting, transferring, conveying, encumbering, or mortgaging immovable property. (2) Receive or draft inventories, appraisements, or partitions. (3) Receive, notarize, or draft wills or prenuptial agreements. (4) Perform any other function of a notary public other than that granted by Subsection A. (5) Draft any documents in the performance of his notary powers".
What is missing: (B)(4), the catch-all. The page reads as if anything not on the "cannot" list is allowed. It is the other way round: only what is on the "can" list is allowed. Also "sells, transfers, or mortgages" is narrower than "effecting, transferring, conveying, encumbering, or mortgaging" (a servitude or lien encumbers without selling).
Secondary: Manual p. 190: "All acts which are performed beyond the specific authority granted to justices of the peace shall be null and void."
Recommendation: change the first item to "notarize any paper that transfers, mortgages, or otherwise affects land or buildings" and add a last item: "do anything else as a notary that is not on the list above".

### 55. Notary fee note
> "Set by the customary practice of the parish. R.S. 13:2586.1(D)."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2586.1(D): "may charge and collect fees for the documents he executes or notarizes in accordance with the customary practice followed in the parish where his court is located."

### 56. Appear, bring identification, sign in front of the justice
> "Every person who signs must appear with a driver's license or passport and sign in front of the Justice of the Peace."

**SUPPORTED** as office practice; it is not presented as a rule of law. Secondary: Manual p. 193 lists the same as best practice ("Ask the parties for identification", "Have all parties appear in the physical presence of the ex officio notary to sign").

## Part 5. About, Contact, Forms, Home, and fixed page text

### 57. Election, term, residence (`about.astro`)
> "A justice of the peace is a judge elected by the voters of a ward. The term is six years. The justice must live in the ward. R.S. 13:2582."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (minor).** R.S. 13:2582, L(77366): "A. Each justice of the peace shall be ... a resident of the ward and district from which elected ... B. Justices of the peace shall be elected at the congressional election for terms of six years by the qualified voters within the territorial limits of their jurisdiction." Subsection G is an exception for Plaquemines Parish.
As a statement about every justice of the peace, "a ward" is too narrow: some courts serve a district, and the statute says "ward and district" and "territorial limits of their jurisdiction". Six years is right. True for Ward 10.
Recommendation: "A justice of the peace is a judge elected by the voters of the court's ward or district. The term is six years. The justice must live there."

### 58. The character of the court (`about.astro`)
> "The court is meant to be simple and near at hand. You may state your case in your own words, the hearing is informal, and the judge's duty is to find the facts and reach a fair decision. C.C.P. art. 4917; art. 4921.2."

**SUPPORTED** for the second sentence (arts. 4917(A), 4921.2(A), quoted in entries 8 and 14; the statute says "impartial determination" and "correct judgment"). The first sentence is the office's description, not a statement of law; neither article says it. It is harmless, but the citations do not support it. Optional: move the citations so they follow only the second sentence, which they already do in substance.

### 59. Civil, eviction, marriage, notary powers (`about.astro`, `index.astro`)
> "decides civil claims of $5,000 or less"; "decides evictions"; "performs marriage ceremonies"; "acts as a notary with limited powers"; "It decides small claims and evictions, performs weddings, and notarizes documents."

**SUPPORTED.** Arts. 4911, 4912; R.S. 9:202; R.S. 13:2586.1.

### 60. Committing magistrate, bail, peace bonds (`about.astro`)
> "serves as a committing magistrate for the parish, with power to set bail in cases that are not capital or necessarily punishable at hard labor, and to require peace bonds. R.S. 13:2586."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2586(C)(1), L(77372): "They shall have criminal jurisdiction parishwide as committing magistrates and shall have the power to bail or discharge, in cases not capital or necessarily punishable at hard labor, and may require bonds to keep the peace." C.Cr.P. art. 314(A)(5), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=112432`: "Justices of the peace in cases not capital or necessarily punishable at hard labor."

### 61. One constable, same voters, same term (`about.astro`)
> "Each justice of the peace court has one constable, elected by the same voters for the same six-year term. R.S. 13:2583."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2583, L(77367): "A. There shall be one constable for the court of each justice of the peace in the several parishes of the state ... B. A constable shall be elected at the congressional election for terms of six years by the qualified electors within the territorial limits of the justice of the peace ward of the court for which he is elected."

### 62. What the constable does (`about.astro`, `contact.astro`)
> "The constable serves the court's papers and enforces its judgments. R.S. 13:2586." "The constable serves the court's papers and carries out its orders."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 13:2586(E)(2): "The constable of the justice of the peace court shall have authority parishwide to enforce a judgment of the justice of the peace court". R.S. 13:2586(F), quoted in entry 11.

### 63. "You do not have to use these forms" (`forms.astro`)
> "You do not have to use these forms. In a justice of the peace court you may state your claim in your own words, in writing or in person. The forms make sure the court gets every fact it needs."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (minor).** Right for a claim and an answer (art. 4917). But the page lists the Notice to Vacate, and that notice must be written (art. 4701: "written notice to vacate"). A landlord could read the paragraph as allowing a spoken notice.
Recommendation: add "The notice to vacate is the exception: the law requires it in writing." Or limit the sentence: "You do not have to use the court's forms."

### 64. Staff limits, footer notice, page disclaimer (`contact.astro`, `Base.astro`, `[service].astro`, `forms.toml`)
> "Staff can tell you how the court works, what a filing costs, and when your hearing is. Staff cannot tell you whether to file, what to say, or how your case will come out. Those are questions for a lawyer." "Court staff cannot give legal advice. This site explains how the court works. It does not replace the law or the advice of a lawyer." "This page is a summary. The law itself controls." Form purposes in `forms.toml`.

**SUPPORTED** (no rule of law asserted beyond the limit on unlicensed advice; consistent with R.S. 37:212 and, as secondary, Manual p. 81: "A justice of the peace cannot give legal advice to the litigants before his court"). The form purpose "It is delivered to the tenant, not filed with the court" is consistent with art. 4701.

## Part 6. The printed forms (`forms/*.typ`)

### 65. Statement of Claim (`civil-claim.typ`)
> "Plus, if claimed: Interest / Court costs / Attorney fees"; "Attach one copy of every document that supports your claim."; "Open account: a sworn statement that the account is correct is attached."; "NSF check: a copy of the written demand and its certified mail receipt is attached."; "The plaintiff affirms that the facts and documents submitted in this claim are true and accurate."

**SUPPORTED.** These are fields and practice, not assertions of law. They are consistent with art. 4911(B) (interest, costs, fees outside the limit), art. 4921(B) ("prima facie proof may be submitted by affidavit" on an open account), and R.S. 9:2782(C)(1), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107207` ("a written demand ... shall be sent by certified or registered mail to the drawer of the check"). The two attachment boxes are optional ticks; neither statute makes the attachment a condition of filing, and the form does not say it is.

### 66. Answer: default warning (`answer.typ`)
> "Use this form to tell the court whether you contest the plaintiff's claim. Do not ignore the citation. If you do not answer or appear in time, the court may give a default judgment against you."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4921(A); art. 4919(A)(5).

### 67. Answer: "every objection and defense"
> "Explain your answer. Include every objection and defense you intend to rely on."

**OVERSTATED.** Same as entry 18. Art. 4917(C) requires "all of the exceptions". Recommendation: "Explain your answer. Include every objection to the suit (exception) you intend to rely on."

### 68. Answer: the consent-to-judgment option
> "I owe the plaintiff what is claimed. I waive any further appearance and delays, and I consent to judgment against me in the amount sought."

**UNCERTAIN.** The wording copies the Attorney General's sample form (secondary: Manual p. 89: "I owe the Plaintiff what he claims, and waive any further appearance and/or delays and consent to judgment against me in the amount sought."). It is not a statement of law, but ticking it is a legal act with effects the form does not explain: the defendant gives up the hearing, and C.C.P. art. 2085 says "An appeal cannot be taken by a party who confessed judgment in the proceedings in the trial court". I found no article in 4911 to 4925 that describes this waiver or its effect in a justice court.
Recommendation: either remove the waiver and consent words and keep only "I owe the plaintiff what is claimed", or keep the option and have the justice approve a one-line warning. Under the office's standard, the first is safer.

### 69. Answer: copy to the plaintiff
> "Send a copy of this answer to the plaintiff. Regular mail is enough."

**UNSUPPORTED.** Nothing in arts. 4917 to 4921.1 requires a defendant in a justice court to send the answer to the plaintiff; art. 4917 lets the answer be oral. The general article, C.C.P. art. 1313(A), `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=111174`, says a later pleading "may be served either by the sheriff or by: (1) Mailing a copy thereof to the counsel of record, or if there is no counsel of record, to the adverse party at his last known address", and art. 1313(B) then requires "a certificate of the manner in which service was made". So "Regular mail is enough" is at best half the rule, and the Manual (p. 65, secondary) says a reconventional demand "must be served by the constable". The Attorney General's sample answer has no such instruction.
Recommendation: omit both sentences. If the office wants plaintiffs to get a copy, say what the office does: "The court will give the plaintiff a copy of your answer."

### 70. Answer: notice to all parties
> "While this case is open, the court will contact you at the address and telephone number you gave. If either changes, tell the court at once."

**SUPPORTED** as office practice (same notice on the Attorney General's sample form, Manual p. 89).

### 71. Notice to Vacate: the notice itself (`notice-to-vacate.typ`)
> "This notice is from the landlord or owner named below. It is not a court order." "You are hereby notified to vacate the premises at the address above within five (5) days of the delivery of this notice to you." "Tenant. Your right to occupy the premises has ended because: ..." "Occupant who is not a tenant. The purpose of your occupancy has ceased because: ..." "Should you fail to vacate within this period, court proceedings will be taken immediately to evict you from the premises under the Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4701 ("lessee's right of occupancy has ceased", "not less than five days from the date of its delivery"); art. 4702 ("after the purpose of the occupancy has ceased", "five days from its delivery").
Limit: this form is a five-day notice to vacate. It is not a notice of termination of a month-to-month lease, which must be given "ten calendar days before the end of that month" (C.C. art. 2728(2)). The box "The lease term has ended" invites a landlord with a month-to-month tenant to use it for that. See Part 8.

### 72. Notice to Vacate: two witness lines
> "Witness" / "Witness" (two signature lines under the landlord's signature)

**UNSUPPORTED** as a requirement. Arts. 4701 to 4703 require a written notice delivered by the lessor or agent. They do not mention witnesses. Two blank witness lines on a court-supplied form suggest the notice is defective without them.
Recommendation: remove them, or label them "Witness to delivery (optional)" and move them to the record-of-delivery page.

### 73. Notice to Vacate: record of delivery
> "Keep this page with a copy of the notice. ... The court will ask how and when the notice was delivered." "Handed to"; "Sent by certified mail (keep the receipt)"; "Attached to a door of the premises, because the premises are abandoned or closed, or the whereabouts of the tenant or occupant is unknown"

**SUPPORTED.** The door option carries the art. 4703 conditions in the statute's words. Art. 4701 fixes no method of delivery other than "cause written notice ... to be delivered"; the Manual (p. 152, secondary) says "The notice may be sent by the landlord or his agent by certified mail or personal service."
Caution on mail: the five days run "from the date of its delivery", not from mailing. "Date delivered" on this page should not be filled with the mailing date. Consider relabelling the mail line: "Sent by certified mail; delivered on (date on the receipt)".

### 74. Notice to Vacate: footnote on counting
> "The five days do not include the day of delivery or legal holidays. C.C.P. arts. 4701, 4702, 4703, and 5059."

**OVERSTATED OR IMPRECISE (material omission).** Same defect as entry 30: weekends are legal holidays for this count (R.S. 1:55(E)(3)) and the note does not say so. Art. 4703 has nothing to do with counting.
Recommendation: "The five days do not include the day of delivery, Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays. C.C.P. arts. 4701, 4702, and 5059; R.S. 1:55."

### 75. Petition of Eviction: fields and representations (`eviction-petition.typ`)
> "Address, in Ward 10"; "a home / business premises or farmland"; "Monthly rent"; "a tenant, written lease / a tenant, verbal agreement / an occupant, not a tenant"; "The defendant waived the five-day notice to vacate in the written lease."; "The plaintiff gave written notice to vacate on (date)"; "sent by certified mail; the receipt is attached"; "The time allowed by the notice has ended and the defendant has not vacated."; "Signature of plaintiff or representative"

**SUPPORTED.** Consistent with art. 4912 (territory, type of premises, rent), art. 4704, L(112076) ("'Lease' means any oral or written lease"), art. 4701 (waiver "contained in the lease"), art. 4731(A) ("fails to comply with the notice to vacate"; "the lessor or owner, or agent thereof").

### 76. Petition of Eviction: "with a witness present"
> "handed to the defendant, with a witness present"; "attached to a door of the premises, with a witness present"

**UNSUPPORTED.** No article in Title XI requires a witness to delivery. A landlord who handed over the notice alone cannot truthfully tick either box, and may think the notice is invalid. Also, the door box omits the condition that makes posting valid (art. 4703: "If the premises are abandoned or closed, or if the whereabouts of the lessee or occupant is unknown").
Recommendation: "handed to the defendant"; "attached to a door of the premises, because the premises were abandoned or closed, or the defendant's whereabouts were unknown"; and, if wanted, a separate optional line "Witness to delivery, if any".

### 77. Petition of Eviction: the request
> "The plaintiff asks that the defendant be ordered to show cause why the defendant should not be ordered to vacate the premises and deliver possession to the plaintiff, and asks for the costs of these proceedings."

**SUPPORTED.** Art. 4731(A): "to show cause why he should not be ordered to deliver possession of the premises to the lessor or owner"; art. 4732(B).

### 78. Wedding Request (`wedding-request.typ`)
> "Give each name exactly as it appears on the marriage license." "The ceremony cannot take place until 24 hours after the license is issued, and must take place within 30 days. Bring the license to the ceremony." "The law requires two adult witnesses at the ceremony." "R.S. 9:205, 9:235, 9:241, and 9:244."

**SUPPORTED.** R.S. 9:241, 9:235, 9:205, 9:244, quoted in entries 46 to 48. The four citations are the right ones. The field "Date and time issued" matches R.S. 9:234, `https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=107068`: "shall show on the face of it the exact time and date of issuance."

### 79. Letterhead and footers (`letterhead.typ`)
> "Court staff cannot give legal advice." "Blank form from the Justice of the Peace Court, Ward 10, St. Tammany Parish, State of Louisiana. Not issued by the court."

**SUPPORTED.** No rule of law asserted. Keeping the court's letterhead off the landlord's notice is sound.

## Part 7. Statements that read as legal advice

These are not wrong in law (unless marked above). They tell a party what to do in that party's own interest, or they invite staff to answer a legal question.

1. **"If you are not sure, ask the office before you file."** (venue, small claims) and **"If you are unsure whether a day counts, ask the office."** (five-day count, evictions). Whether venue is proper, and whether a notice period has run, are questions the justice may have to decide between the parties. An answer from the office before filing is advice to one side on a contested point, and it contradicts the Contact page ("Staff cannot tell you whether to file"). Recommendation: state the rule and cite it; drop the invitation, or send the reader to a lawyer.
2. **"Tell the court your defense. To keep the right to stay in the property during an appeal, your answer must be made under oath and must state a defense that entitles you to stay."** Accurate (art. 4735), but phrased as tactical guidance to the tenant. Neutral form: "An appeal suspends the eviction only if the tenant answered under oath with a defense that entitles the tenant to stay, and asks for the appeal and files the bond within 24 hours of the judgment. C.C.P. art. 4735."
3. **"Your answer must include every objection and defense you intend to rely on."** (page and form). Advice, and overstated (entries 18, 67).
4. **The consent-to-judgment option on the Answer.** A legal act offered without its consequences (entry 68).
5. **"Send a copy of this answer to the plaintiff. Regular mail is enough."** An instruction on how to serve a pleading (entry 69).
6. **"Count the five days correctly."** Fine as a heading, but the content beneath must then be complete (entry 30).
7. **"Do not ignore the citation." "Come to the hearing." "Bring your evidence."** Procedural information of the kind the Attorney General's public guide also gives (AG Guide p. 5). Acceptable.
8. **"If the other party does not pay, ask the office what steps the court can take."** Acceptable if staff confine the answer to what filings exist and what they cost.

## Part 8. Where a resident could be misled by omission

1. **Weekends in the five-day count.** Entries 30 and 74. The most likely cause of a failed eviction.
2. **Housing covered by federal law.** The site presents five days as the notice for every tenant. For a "covered dwelling" (for example a property with a federally backed mortgage or in a federal housing program), 15 U.S.C. 9058(c) says the lessor "may not require the tenant to vacate the covered dwelling unit before the date that is 30 days after the date on which the lessor provides the tenant with a notice to vacate" (text read at law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/9058, not an official source; I did not verify how courts now apply it). The Manual (pp. 156 to 160, secondary, 2020) also gives different notice rules for public and subsidized housing. Recommendation: one sentence, "Federal law may require a longer notice for subsidized housing and for property with a federally backed mortgage. Ask a lawyer."
3. **Ending a month-to-month lease with the five-day form.** Entries 36 and 71. The Notice to Vacate form gives five days and has a box "The lease term has ended". A lease with no fixed term ends only by a written notice of termination given ten calendar days before the end of the month (C.C. arts. 2727 to 2729). Recommendation: on the form or the page, "To end a lease that has no fixed end date, the landlord must first give the written notice of termination that C.C. art. 2728 requires."
4. **Mail and the delivery date.** Entry 73. The period runs from delivery.
5. **Witnesses.** Entries 72 and 76. The forms imply a requirement that does not exist.
6. **Eviction appeals.** The evictions page explains when an appeal stops the eviction but not where or when to appeal. Arts. 4924 and 4925 apply (15 days, trial de novo in the 22nd Judicial District Court). A tenant could think the 24-hour deadline is the only deadline, or that no appeal is possible after it.
7. **The notary catch-all.** Entry 54.
8. **Businesses and assigned claims.** R.S. 37:212(C): a business may act through an officer or employee only on its own claim of $5,000 or less, and not on "any claim assigned to it". The site says "no lawyer is required" without this.
9. **Proceeding without paying costs.** Individuals only; two affidavits, one from a third person (art. 5183); costs can still be assessed against the loser (art. 5188). "Without paying in advance" is accurate, but a reader may hear "free".
10. **The appeal is final and is not capped.** Art. 4924(B): the trial de novo "is not subject to the jurisdictional limit of the justice of the peace court"; art. 4924(C): "No further appeal". A motion for new trial changes when the 15 days start (art. 4925(B)).
11. **Consent to judgment.** Entry 68.
12. **Fifteen-day answer delay** when served through the Secretary of State (art. 4920). Harmless, since the citation states the delay.
13. **A justice court cannot issue injunctions** (art. 4913(C)) and there is no jury (Manual p. 55, citing art. 4871, which I did not fetch). People sometimes ask a justice court to order a neighbor to stop something.
14. **Minors.** No marriage under 16; at 16 or 17, a court's authorization and parental consent, and no license if the age gap is three years or more (R.S. 9:221(B); Ch.C. art. 1545). "Other rules apply" is safe; the detail is optional.
15. **Manual is out of date in places.** Fees (2017 schedule), minors' marriages (pre-2019), notice of judgment under art. 4922 (amended 2026). Do not use it to settle those points.
